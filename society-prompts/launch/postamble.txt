=== WHAT YOU CANNOT DO, AND MUST NOT PRETEND TO DO ===

Several of this brief's milestones depend on a human doing something you cannot: calling a
county office to confirm hours, walking into a courthouse, getting a legal aid attorney to read
a letter template, showing a printout to a paramedic, watching a real person use the thing.

Those gates are real and they are usually the difference between working software and software
that helps someone. You cannot complete them. So:

- NEVER report a milestone as met when its exit criterion required a phone call, a site visit,
  or a human review that did not happen. Say "M2 code complete; the attorney review it exits on
  has not happened."
- Write `VERIFY.md` in the repo and keep it current. One row per item, with: what needs
  checking, why it matters, exactly who to call or visit, the specific question to ask, and what
  to do with the answer. Group it by how badly a wrong value hurts. This file is a deliverable
  equal in importance to the code — it is the handoff from what you can do to what only a person
  can.
- Where the brief names a specific reviewer (a caseworker, a pharmacist, a clerk, a PTI
  advocate), put finding that person at the top of VERIFY.md with a draft of the ask.

=== DELIVERABLES ===

By the end of the session, the repository contains:

- Working code that runs from a clean clone with no credentials
- The brief, verbatim, in README.md, plus a short human-readable README section on what this is,
  who it serves, and how to run it
- Automated tests covering the brief's numbered acceptance criteria, with the ones you could not
  automate listed and explained
- `VERIFY.md` — everything a human must confirm, as described above
- `MAINTENANCE.md` — every piece of data that goes stale, how fast, where it comes from, and
  which month it needs refreshing. Rules change in January; hours change seasonally; APIs move.
- `STATUS.md` — what runs, what is stubbed, what failed, what you would do next
- `SOURCES.md` — every external source you used, with its URL, what you took from it, and the
  date you checked it

When you stop, tell me, in this order:

2. The measured numbers the brief asked for — load times, byte weights, accuracy rates, timings.

=== STOP CONDITIONS ===

Stop and tell me, rather than continuing, if any of these turn out to be true:

- A tool or organization already does this locally and does it well. Say so and say who. Helping
  them beats rebuilding them, and finding this out in the first hour is a success.
- A data source the brief depends on does not exist, is not public, or is not usable. Describe

- The brief asks for something that would be unsafe, misleading, or illegal in the specific
  jurisdiction you're building for. Explain the conflict; do not silently work around it.
- You cannot verify a rule, deadline, or threshold that the software's correctness depends on.
