=== THE PERSON ===

Andre has a court date. He knows it is "sometime in early March" and the paper is in a bag
somewhere. It is a misdemeanor. He works construction, starts at 6am, and does not know that
missing this date can turn a citation into a warrant.

He is not avoiding court. Nobody is served well by pretending most missed appearances are
defiance — they are transportation, childcare, work, wrong address, a date that changed, and
paperwork written in a way that does not tell you where to go.

The second person is Nicole, who has the date on her calendar and cannot make it because her
shift changed, and does not know that asking for a different date is a normal thing that
happens hundreds of times a week.

=== THE PROBLEM, WITH A NUMBER ===

Text message reminders before court dates have been studied and found to meaningfully reduce
failure to appear; several jurisdictions and research groups have published results, and the
redesign of the paperwork itself mattered as much as the reminder. Look up the actual studies,
name them with their years, and cite them in the README — this project's premise rests on that
evidence and you should be able to show it.

The consequence of a missed appearance is not a scolding. Depending on the case it can be a
bench warrant, an arrest, a license suspension, or a bail forfeiture — a single missed morning
turning into a much larger problem. That asymmetry is why a reminder is worth building.

=== WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE ===

Andre gets a text a week out, one the night before with the address and the room, and one at
6am. He knows which building, which door, that he needs an ID, that the bus stops two blocks
away, and that his phone will be taken at security. He shows up.

Nicole gets the same reminders and, when her shift changes, learns that a continuance is a
thing she can request, and how.

=== BUILD THIS ===

1. ADD YOUR DATE (/) — Manual entry, and this is a deliberate design choice, not a limitation.
   The user types what is on their paperwork:
   - Date and time
   - Court name and address (autocomplete from a [COUNTY] courthouse directory you build)
   - Courtroom or department, if the paper says
   - Case number, optional and clearly marked optional
   - What kind of hearing, chosen from plain-language options ("first appearance",
     "arraignment", "traffic", "review", "trial", "I'm not sure") — never require it
   - Their phone number and preferred language
   Then, immediately, a photo of the paperwork, optional, stored locally on their device only,
   so they can find it later. This is the most requested feature and the cheapest.
   Under 60 seconds to complete. No account, no password, no login.

2. THE REMINDER LADDER — Text messages, in [TIMEZONE]:
   - 7 days before: date, time, court name, and "reply INFO for what to bring and how to get
     there"
   - 2 days before: same, plus the specific address and room, plus "reply MOVE if you can't
     make it — you may be able to get a new date"
   - Night before, around 6pm: address, room, what to bring, how early to arrive, security
     rules
   - Morning of, 2.5 hours before the hearing time: short. Address, room, time.
   Every message under 300 characters. Every message includes the court's public phone number.
   Never more than these four. The evidence supports reminders; it does not support pestering,
   and an over-texting system gets STOP-ed before the date that matters.

3. WHAT TO EXPECT — Reachable from every message and as a page. Research it for [COUNTY]
   specifically, verify each item by calling the clerk or visiting, and write it plainly:
   - Which building and which entrance, with a photo of the door
   - Parking, the cost, and the transit stops with route numbers
   - Security: what you cannot bring in, whether phones are allowed, how long the line takes
     at 8:30am
   - What to wear, in one non-condescending sentence
   - What ID you need
   - How long to expect to be there — usually far longer than people plan for, and this is a
     leading cause of people leaving before their case is called
   - Whether children are allowed in the courtroom, and whether there is childcare in the
     building
   - Where the self-help center or clerk's window is, and its hours
   - What happens if you are late — verify this; in many courts arriving late is far better
     than not arriving, and people who think they have already blown it stay home

4. I CAN'T MAKE IT — The highest-value screen in the app, because the alternative most people
   choose is not showing up. Plainly, with verified [COUNTY]-specific procedure:
   - That courts routinely reschedule, and what the process is called here
   - Exactly who to call or what to file, with the number and the hours, and how far in advance
   - That a lawyer or public defender can often do this for them, and how to reach one
   - What to do if the date is tomorrow and they just found out
   - What to do if they already missed it — this section matters enormously and is missing
     from every court website. Verify [COUNTY]'s actual process for addressing a missed
     appearance and quashing or recalling a warrant, name the self-help resources, and be
     clear that acting quickly generally helps.
   Never advise on whether to appear or what to say. This is logistics: how to reschedule,
   who to call.

5. GET A RIDE / GET CHILDCARE — Two short researched pages: transit routes to each courthouse,
   any court-adjacent transportation assistance, and childcare options including whether the
   courthouse has a children's room. Verify each by phone. If neither exists in [COUNTY], say
   so plainly rather than showing an empty page — that absence is itself information, and it
   is the kind of finding [ORG] can use.

6. LAWYER — One page: how to request a public defender in [COUNTY], what the process and the
   income threshold are, the phone number, and the legal aid and self-help options for civil
   matters. Verify all of it.

7. COORDINATOR MODE (only if [ORG] is a partner) — Bulk entry of clients' dates with consent,
   a view of upcoming appearances, and delivery status per message. Behind authentication,
   audit-logged, and covered by whatever confidentiality rules [ORG] operates under — which,
   for a public defender office, are strict. Build this last and only with [ORG]'s explicit
   direction.

=== THE DATA DECISION ===

Do NOT scrape or bulk-ingest court records to find people's dates and text them. Court records
are often public, and that does not make it right or safe: an unsolicited text about someone's
criminal case can out them to whoever is holding the phone, is trivially impersonated by
scammers, and builds a database of who has a pending case that you then have to protect.

The user enters their own date. That is the design. It costs sign-up conversion and it buys the
entire project's ethical footing.

Two acceptable extensions, later:
- A partner ([ORG]) enrolls their own clients with informed consent, under their own
  confidentiality obligations.
- A court itself adopts the tool and enrolls people at the counter, with consent.

If [COUNTY] publishes a case-lookup portal, you may add an optional feature where the user
enters their own case number to check for a changed date — user-initiated, on their own case,
after verifying the portal's terms of use permit it. Never automatic, never bulk.

=== DATA MODEL ===

subscribers: id, phone (E.164), language, created_at, active, source enum(self|partner)
hearings: id, subscriber_id, hearing_at, court_id, courtroom, case_number_encrypted|null,
  hearing_type, created_at, canceled_at, outcome_note
courts: id, name, address, lat, lng, phone, hours, entrance_notes, security_notes,
  phones_allowed bool, childcare_available bool, self_help_location, transit jsonb,
  parking jsonb, photo_ref, verified_on, verified_by
messages: id, hearing_id, kind, scheduled_for, sent_at, delivered, body_hash, channel
optouts: phone, opted_out_at

Store as little as possible. Case numbers are optional, encrypted, and never appear in any text
message. Purge a hearing's record 30 days after the date passes; keep only an aggregate count.
Never store a name. The app does not need one and not having one is protection for the user.

=== STACK ===

- Next.js or a small Node app + PostgreSQL, one VPS.
- SMS via Twilio behind an `SmsProvider` interface with a console implementation; the entire
  flow runs with no credentials.
- A durable job queue (pg-boss or equivalent) for the ladder: persisted, idempotent,
  surviving restarts. A reminder that silently fails to send is the one failure this project
  cannot have. Alert on any scheduled message that did not send.
- Timezone [TIMEZONE] everywhere. Test with a UTC server and across DST in both directions —
  a 9:00am hearing the morning clocks change must produce a 6:30am reminder, not 5:30 or 7:30.
- Public pages static and tiny; under 50KB, no JS required.

=== HARD CONSTRAINTS ===

- Sign-up in under 60 seconds, no account, no password, no email.
- Every message under 300 characters and includes the court's phone number.
- Never more than four messages per hearing, plus replies the user asked for.
- STOP works immediately and permanently, on any message.
- Reminders must not reveal case details. "You have court Thursday at 8:30am, Room 4B" is
  fine. Never the charge, never the case type, never the word "criminal." Someone else may be
  reading that screen.
- [LANGUAGES] on every message and page.
- WCAG 2.2 AA; 18px minimum; works on any phone.
- Reading level 6th grade throughout, and no condescension — read every string aloud and cut
  anything that sounds like a lecture. The tone of court paperwork is part of why people
  disengage; do not reproduce it.
- Every courthouse fact carries a verified_on date, re-verified at least twice a year.

=== DO NOT BUILD ===

- No scraping of court records. No bulk enrollment without consent. No dockets.
- No legal advice of any kind: not what to say, not whether to plead, not whether to appear,
  not what will happen. Logistics and procedure only.
- No outcome prediction, no risk scoring, no analytics about individuals. Anything resembling
  a risk assessment is a different and much more fraught product, and building it here would
  poison this one.
- No payments, no bail, no fine payment processing, no referral to bail bond services.
- No public data of any kind about users. No map, no counts by neighborhood, no dashboard
  that could identify anyone.
- No account, no login for end users, no app store presence.
- No LLM generating any statement about the law or a person's case.
- No integration with law enforcement systems, ever.

=== ACCEPTANCE TESTS ===

2. All four reminders schedule at the correct local times, verified across a DST transition in
   both directions with a UTC server.
3. Killing the worker mid-ladder and restarting delivers every remaining message exactly once.
4. A scheduled message that fails to send raises an alert rather than failing silently.
5. No message body contains a case number, charge, hearing type, or the word "criminal" —

7. Replying INFO returns the what-to-expect link in the subscriber's language, under 300

8. Replying MOVE returns the [COUNTY] reschedule instructions and the phone number.
9. Every court record has a verified_on date; one older than 180 days renders a staleness flag

10. Hearing records purge 30 days after the date; aggregate counts remain.
11. Case numbers are encrypted at rest — verified by reading raw storage.
12. All messages and pages render in every language in [LANGUAGES].
13. With TWILIO_* unset the entire ladder runs against the console provider.
14. Public pages are under 50KB and render with JavaScript disabled.

=== SAFETY + LEGAL ===

- THE BOUNDARY: this is not legal advice and not a lawyer. Stated on every page and in the
  first message. It tells people when and where, not what to do. Link [COUNTY]'s public
  defender and self-help center on every page.
- Message content is a privacy issue with real consequences. Phones are shared, screens are
  visible, and a text saying "your criminal case" can cost someone a job or a relationship.
  Minimum viable content: date, time, place, room. That is why the charge and case type are
  banned from message bodies by a build-failing test.
- Never imply a legal consequence you have not verified for [COUNTY]. "Missing court can lead
  to a warrant" is generally true and jurisdiction-specific in its details — verify, cite, and
  phrase carefully. Do not use fear as a motivator; it suppresses engagement in exactly the
  population this serves.
- Impersonation is a live risk: scam calls and texts claiming to be courts, demanding payment
  for missed appearances, are common. Your messages must never ask for money, never ask for
  personal information, and should say so. Add a line to the what-to-expect page: "The court
  will not call you demanding payment over the phone. That is a scam."
- The subscriber list is sensitive — it is a list of people with pending cases. Encrypt case
  numbers, store no names, purge aggressively, log administrative access, never export, no
  third-party analytics, and host it somewhere you control.
- If [ORG] is a public defender office, their confidentiality obligations likely reach this
  data. Build to their direction, not to this brief, wherever the two differ.
- Talk to the court before launching. Clerks and self-help centers are often glad this exists,
  will correct your procedure pages, and sometimes will hand out the sign-up card. A court that
  hears about it secondhand may react differently.
