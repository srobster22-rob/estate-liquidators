=== THE PEOPLE ===

Elena has two children, 6 and 11, both US citizens. She works a shift that ends at 4. She has
thought about what would happen if she were detained during that shift, and she has not written
anything down, because writing it down makes it real and because she does not know where such a
list would be safe.

What she needs is mundane and specific: someone who can legally pick her kids up from school
tomorrow, someone who knows where the birth certificates are, a phone number for a lawyer that
is not a notario, and a sentence to say if someone knocks.

The second person is her sister Rosa, a citizen, who has agreed to be that person and has no idea
what agreeing entails.

The third is the school secretary, who can only release a child to someone on a form.

=== THE ARCHITECTURE OF NOT KNOWING ===

This app must be built so that it cannot betray anyone, because the threat model is a government
with subpoena power and a phone that may be searched.

1. **No server. None.** Not for accounts, not for backups, not for analytics, not for error
   reporting. Everything is local and the app says so on the first screen in plain words in every
   supported language.
2. **Never ask about immigration status.** Not the user's, not anyone's. The plan is the same
   regardless and asking creates a record that should not exist. Put a visible line on the first
   screen: "This app never asks about anyone's immigration status."
3. **No identifiers you do not need.** A-numbers, passport numbers, and case numbers are exactly
   what a plan needs to be useful and exactly what is most dangerous. Store them only where the
   user opts in per field, encrypted, behind a lock, and say plainly what the trade-off is.
4. **A lock by default**, an unremarkable app name and icon, and a fast exit. The phone may be
   handed to someone.
5. **The most important output is paper.** A printed card in a wallet and a printed plan in a
   trusted person's house work when a phone is confiscated, dead, or locked. Design for paper
   first and the screen second.

Write these five into the README as standing constraints and test what can be tested.

=== THE PROBLEM ===

Family preparedness planning is standard, recommended practice from immigrant legal services
organizations. The gap is not that the idea is unknown; it is that doing it requires a dozen
specific steps — a caregiver authorization the school will accept, documents gathered in one
place, someone briefed — and there is no checklist that fits a phone and a Tuesday.

Research and verify from [STATE] sources and from accredited legal organizations:
- What [STATE] provides for delegating caregiving authority to another adult without going to
  court. Many states have a short affidavit or caregiver authorization form for school and
  medical decisions; verify what [STATE] has, what it does and does not cover, whether it needs
  notarization, and how long it lasts.
- What [CITY]'s school district requires to release a child to someone other than a parent, and
  how to add an emergency contact. Call and ask.
- Whether [STATE] has any standby guardianship provision.
- The know-your-rights content: what a warrant signed by a judge is and how it differs from an
  administrative form, the right to remain silent, the right not to open the door, and the right
  to speak to a lawyer. Take this from a national immigrant rights organization or a public
  defender office and quote it with attribution — do not compose it.
- How to find accredited help: the Department of Justice recognized-organization and accredited-
  representative list, and the [STATE] bar's immigration referral. Verify both.
- The detainee locator, and how a family member finds someone.

=== WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE ===

Elena spends forty minutes on a Sunday. Rosa is on the school's pickup list and has a signed
caregiver form. The documents are in one envelope and Rosa knows which shelf. Both of them have a
card in their wallet with two phone numbers on it. Nothing about any of this exists on anybody's
server.

=== BUILD THIS ===

1. START (/) — Three sentences, then the checklist. First sentence: nothing you enter leaves this
   phone. Second: this app never asks about anyone's immigration status. Third: the most useful
   thing you can do today takes ten minutes.
   No onboarding, no explanation of why this matters. The person opening this app knows why.

2. THE CHECKLIST — Ordered by protection-per-minute, each with a real time estimate:
   - **Name the person** who can care for your children, and confirm they agree. (5 min)
   - **Put them on the school's pickup and emergency contact list.** The specific form, where to
     get it, and that a phone call is usually not enough. (15 min, requires going to the office)
   - **Complete [STATE]'s caregiver authorization form**, if it has one, and give a copy to that
     person and to the school. Note whether notarization is needed. (30 min)
   - **Gather the documents** into one place and tell the person where. (30 min — see part 4)
   - **Memorize or carry two phone numbers**: the caregiver, and a legal organization. (5 min)
   - **Write down the children's basics**: full legal names, dates of birth, school, grade,
     teacher, allergies, medications, doctor. Rosa will be asked all of this.
   - **Decide about money**: whether anyone else can access an account, and how rent gets paid.
     Note plainly that this has legal consequences and to ask a lawyer about anything beyond a
     joint account.
   - **Practice the door conversation** (part 3).
   Each item is markable done, and the checklist prints.

3. AT THE DOOR — Quoted from a national immigrant rights organization or public defender office,
   with attribution, in [LANGUAGES]:
   - You do not have to open the door.
   - Ask them to show identification and any warrant through a window or under the door.
   - A search warrant signed by a judge has specific features; an administrative form is not the
     same thing. Explain how to tell, quoting the source.
   - You have the right to remain silent, and to say you want to speak to a lawyer.
   - Do not sign anything without talking to a lawyer.
   - Do not lie and do not carry false documents.
   Printable as a wallet card in every supported language, one side per language, large type. The
   card is the deliverable; the screen is the draft.
   VERIFY this content against a current primary source and attribute it. If you cannot, ship
   only the two phone numbers and the sentence "I want to speak to a lawyer. I am going to remain
   silent." — which is short, correct, and enough.

4. THE DOCUMENT LIST — What to gather, not the documents themselves. For each: what it is, why it
   matters, where to get a replacement, and a place to note where the original is kept.
   Birth certificates for everyone, passports, any immigration documents and their numbers, social
   security cards, marriage certificate, lease or deed, insurance, medical records and vaccination
   records for the children, school records, bank information, and the caregiver form.
   Scanning is offered, off by default, with an honest explanation: a scan on the phone is
   convenient and is also a copy of everything on a device that could be searched. Default to
   recording locations rather than storing images, and let the user choose otherwise knowingly.

5. THE PLAN CARD — One printed page for the caregiver, generated from what the user entered:
   children's details, school and pickup instructions, medical basics, where the documents are,
   the legal organization's number, and the detainee locator with instructions. Plus a second
   wallet card for the user: two phone numbers and the door script.
   Print both. Tell them to give one to the caregiver on paper and to keep one somewhere that is
   not the phone.

6. IF IT HAPPENS — A calm, short page: how to find someone through the detainee locator, how to
   reach the legal organization, what the caregiver should do in the first 24 hours, what the
   school needs, and a plain statement that the family has the right to a lawyer but not to a
   free one in immigration proceedings — verify this and state it accurately, because the
   expectation gap here is cruel.

7. FINDING REAL HELP — A verified directory: DOJ-recognized organizations and accredited
   representatives serving [COUNTY], immigration legal aid clinics, and the [STATE] bar referral.
   With a prominent, plainly-written warning about notario fraud: in many countries a "notario"
   is a licensed attorney and in the US a notary public is not, and people lose money and cases
   to this constantly. Explain how to check whether someone is authorized to practice, with the
   official lookup. Verify every organization by phone.

=== SAFETY + LEGAL ===

- Not legal advice, and immigration law is an area where general information is frequently wrong
  for a specific person. Every screen names the accredited organization and its phone number.
- The unauthorized practice of immigration law is a real harm with a specific name — notario
  fraud — and this app must be structurally incapable of contributing to it: no filings, no
  eligibility screening, no case-specific guidance, and no listing of anyone unaccredited.
- Never state or imply that any particular preparation protects anyone from any outcome.
- Be accurate about the right to counsel in immigration proceedings; overstating it sets families
  up for a devastating surprise.
- The data-minimization rules are not privacy preferences, they are the safety design. Do not add
  a server "just for backups," do not add analytics "just to see usage," and do not add crash
  reporting. Write that reasoning into the README so the next maintainer does not undo it.
- Distribute through trusted organizations rather than open channels where possible. A tool like
  this reaches people through someone they already trust, and it is also the kind of tool that
  gets impersonated — a fake version is a real risk, so the real one should be pointed to by the
  organizations themselves.
- Do not display any statistic about enforcement or detention. It is not needed and it dates fast.
