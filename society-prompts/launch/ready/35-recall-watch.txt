=== THE PEOPLE ===

Bernice is 74 and buys the same eleven things every week. A brand of frozen vegetables she eats
twice a week was recalled for listeria in March. She learned about it in May, from a neighbour.
She does not watch the news and does not use social media, and the recall notice at the store
entrance was on a sheet of paper at knee height.

The second person is Dana, who runs a food pantry at [ORG] and has 400 cases of donated product
on pallets. A recall for her is an operational emergency and she currently finds out the same way
Bernice does.

The third is Marcus, who has a car with an open safety recall he has never heard about, which is
free to fix.

=== THE PROBLEM ===

Recalls are announced through federal feeds and press releases, and the people most exposed are
the least likely to see them. Look up current figures on recall awareness and consumer response
rates — FDA, USDA FSIS, NHTSA, and CPSC all publish relevant material — and cite anything you
display with a year.

The operational gap: the information is public, structured, and free, and there is no mechanism
that connects it to the specific person who has the product on a shelf.

=== THE ALERT DISCIPLINE ===

The failure mode of this project is not missing a recall. It is sending Bernice fourteen texts,
after which she ignores the fifteenth, which is the one about the vegetables.

Four rules:

1. **Never alert on a guess.** A match is a match on the specific product, brand, and where
   possible the lot, UPC, or date range in the recall notice. A brand-level fuzzy match is a
   candidate for review, not an alert.
2. **One message per recall per subscriber.** Ever. Deduplicate by (subscriber, recall_id) at the
   database layer, not in application logic.
3. **Severity gates the channel.** The highest-severity food recalls — the class where reasonable
   probability of serious harm is indicated — get a text. Everything else gets a weekly digest,
   or nothing. Verify the current classification schemes from FDA and USDA and encode them as
   data.
4. **Silence is a feature.** A week with no message is the correct outcome most weeks and the app
   should say so at signup, so silence does not read as breakage.

=== WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE ===

Bernice gets four texts a year. One of them is about the vegetables, on the day it is announced,
and it says what to do with the bag in her freezer.

=== BUILD THIS ===

1. INGEST — Poll the public federal recall sources. Research and verify each before building:
   - **FDA** food and drug enforcement/recall data, including the openFDA enforcement endpoints
     and the FDA recall press release feed.
   - **USDA FSIS** recalls for meat, poultry, and egg products, which are a separate system from
     FDA and cover different foods — a household does not know or care about the distinction, so
     the app must cover both.
   - **CPSC** for consumer products, including children's products.
   - **NHTSA** for vehicles and child car seats, which supports lookup by VIN.
   VERIFY each endpoint exists, what it returns, its rate limits, and its terms before building
   on it. Record what you verified.
   Build a `RecallSource` interface with one implementation per feed plus a `FixtureSource` that
   replays recorded responses from disk, so the entire pipeline runs and is testable with no
   network and no credentials. That fixture path is not a testing convenience — it is what lets
   somebody develop this on a laptop and what makes the matching logic verifiable.
   Ingest must be **idempotent**: a recall seen twice creates one row. Key on the source's own
   identifier plus the source name. Re-running yesterday's poll must change nothing.

2. THE WATCH LIST — What a subscriber tells you they have. Entry must be easy or nobody does it:
   - Type a brand and product in plain words. Autocomplete over what has appeared in past recalls
     helps, but free text must always work.
   - Scan or type a UPC. A UPC is the strongest match key you can get from a household.
   - For vehicles: a VIN, checked against NHTSA on entry and then on a schedule. A VIN lookup
     that finds an existing open recall on day one is the single most likely immediate win in the
     whole app.
   - Categories as a coarse fallback: "infant formula," "ground beef," "romaine," "children's
     toys." Coarse watches produce more candidates and are opt-in with that explained.
   Nothing else. No shopping history import, no receipt scanning, no loyalty card integration —
   see DO NOT BUILD.

3. THE MATCHER — The heart of it, and the part to test hardest.
   - Exact match: UPC in the recall notice matches a watched UPC. Alert.
   - Strong match: brand plus product name both match, and the recall's lot codes or date range
     either match what the subscriber recorded or the recall specifies none. Alert.
   - Candidate: brand matches, product is fuzzy, or the subscriber has only a category watch.
     Goes to a review queue for a human at [ORG], never straight to a text.
   - No match: nothing happens, and that is the common case.
   Normalize aggressively before comparing: case, punctuation, "oz"/"ounce", brand suffixes like
   "Inc" and "LLC". Write the normalizer as its own module with its own tests, because every
   false positive and every miss traces back to it.
   Every match records why it matched, which fields, and at what confidence. A match you cannot
   explain is a bug.

4. NOTIFY — SMS as the primary channel, email as an option, behind an `SmsProvider` interface
   with a console implementation so the whole system runs with no credentials.
   The message: what the product is, what the problem is in six words, what to do (throw away /
   return for refund / stop using / see a doctor if symptoms), and a short link. Under 300
   characters. In the subscriber's language.
   Delivery is a **durable, idempotent job**: persisted, retried with backoff, surviving a
   restart, and keyed so a redelivery attempt cannot double-send. A notification pipeline that
   loses messages on deploy is the failure this project cannot have, and one that double-sends
   trains people to ignore it.
   STOP unsubscribes immediately and permanently.

5. THE REVIEW QUEUE — For candidates. [ORG]'s coordinator sees the recall, the watch item, why it
   was flagged, and three buttons: alert / not a match / need more info. Fifteen seconds each.
   This is the human check that keeps precision high without discarding coarse watches, and it is
   also how the matcher's thresholds get tuned — record every decision and report the false
   positive rate.

6. THE PANTRY VIEW — For Dana. A bulk watch list by brand and category, a dashboard of open
   recalls affecting her inventory, and a printable pull sheet: product, lot codes, what to do
   with it, and the recall notice to post. Food banks handle recalls constantly and mostly on
   paper; this view is an afternoon of work and may be the most-used thing you build.

7. PUBLIC PAGE — A no-signup page listing current high-severity recalls for food, drugs, and
   children's products, plainly written, with the source and date on each. Someone who heard
   something on the news should be able to check in ten seconds without giving you a phone number.

=== DATA MODEL ===

recalls: id, source enum(fda|fsis|cpsc|nhtsa), source_ref, title, brands text[],
  products text[], upcs text[], lot_codes text[], date_range, hazard, severity, remedy,

matches: id, recall_id, watch_item_id, confidence enum(exact|strong|candidate), reason jsonb,
  created_at, decided_at, decision enum(alert|not_a_match|unclear), decided_by
notifications: id, subscriber_id, recall_id, channel, scheduled_for, sent_at, delivered,
  idempotency_key
  UNIQUE (subscriber_id, recall_id)   -- one message per recall per person, at the database layer

  persisted.

- Timezone [TIMEZONE]; digests send in the morning local time; tested against a UTC server and

=== HARD CONSTRAINTS ===

- Ingest is idempotent, enforced by a unique constraint. Re-running a poll changes nothing.
- One notification per subscriber per recall, enforced by a unique constraint.
- No alert on a candidate match without a human decision.
- Every match stores its reason. Unexplainable matches fail review.
- Delivery jobs survive a process restart, exactly once.
- Messages under 300 characters, in the subscriber's language, with a STOP instruction.
- Subscribers give a phone number and their watch list. Nothing else — no name, no address, no
  date of birth.
- Public page under 60KB, no JS required.
- WCAG 2.2 AA; 18px minimum; the public page printable.

=== DO NOT BUILD ===

- No loyalty card, grocery account, or purchase history integration. It is the obvious way to
  populate a watch list and it means holding a household's complete shopping record — which
  reveals pregnancy, illness, addiction, and religion. Not worth it, and the value is marginal
  over a typed list of eleven things.
- No receipt scanning or photo-of-your-pantry recognition in v1.
- No health advice. The message says what the recall notice says to do and, where the notice does,
  "see a doctor if you have symptoms." Nothing more.
- No LLM deciding whether something matches. Deterministic rules with recorded reasons, plus a
  human for the ambiguous ones. A hallucinated match sends a false alarm; a hallucinated non-match
  costs the entire point of the project.
- No LLM rewriting recall text. Hazard descriptions are precise and legally drafted.
- No advertising, no affiliate links to replacement products, no partnerships with brands or
  retailers.
- No public database of which households own what.
- No scraping of retailer sites; federal feeds only.
- No account passwords for subscribers. Phone number identity, signed links from texts.

=== ACCEPTANCE TESTS ===

1. Re-running a full ingest over the same fixture data creates zero new rows and zero new matches.
2. A recall appearing in two feeds under different identifiers creates two rows and does not
   double-notify a subscriber matched by both — the unique constraint on (subscriber, recall) is
   per recall, so this test must assert the dedup behaviour you chose and document it.
3. A UPC match produces `exact` and is alerted without human review.
4. A brand-only match produces `candidate` and is never sent without a decision.
5. A recall specifying lot codes does not strongly match a watch item whose recorded lot is
   outside them.
6. The normalizer collapses case, punctuation, unit spellings, and corporate suffixes — tested
   against at least 20 real pairs.
7. Two concurrent notification attempts for the same (subscriber, recall) result in exactly one
   send.
8. Killing the worker mid-delivery and restarting sends every remaining message exactly once.
9. The whole pipeline runs against `FixtureSource` and `ConsoleSmsProvider` with no network and
   no credentials.
10. Messages are under 300 characters and render in the subscriber's language.
11. STOP halts everything immediately, including already-queued messages.
12. A subscriber record contains no name, address, or date of birth — schema assertion.
13. Digests send at the correct local morning time across a DST transition with a UTC server.
14. The public page renders under 60KB with JavaScript disabled.

=== SAFETY + LEGAL ===

- Not health or safety advice. Messages relay what the official notice says to do and link it.
  Never add an instruction the notice does not contain.
- Never suppress or soften a hazard description. If a notice says a product may cause serious
  illness or death, the message says so.
- Under-alerting and over-alerting are both harms and they trade against each other. The design
  resolves toward precision — because an ignored channel protects nobody — and that choice must
  be written in a code comment where the thresholds live, so the next maintainer knows it was
  deliberate.
- Federal recall data is public. Republish it faithfully, always link the original, and never
  alter a hazard statement.
- Do not name a retailer as selling a recalled product unless the official notice does.
- The subscriber list is a list of what people have in their homes. Minimum data, no analytics, no
  export, no sharing, and a documented retention limit.
- Delivery failures are safety-relevant. Alert an operator when a send fails permanently rather
  than dropping it silently.
- Do not display any recall-awareness statistic you have not sourced with a year.
